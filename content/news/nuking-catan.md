## What Nukes actually is

Nukes is a cold-war territory control game on a modular hex map. No dice. No cards. No luck. Every outcome is the direct result of a decision you made.

The core mechanic: units are fluid. A single token is Infantry. Two tokens stacked together become Artillery. Three become Airborne. Four or more become a Base. The same physical pieces change behaviour based purely on how you group them:

- **Infantry** floods through same-biome regions like water filling a container
- **Artillery** jumps in straight lines over occupied hexes
- **Airborne** slingshots exactly two regions, skipping what is between
- **Bases** do not move, but convert isotopes and boost passing platoons

Win by destroying an opponent's city via nuclear strike, establishing a Base on their city, or being the last player with units on the board.

## The hostage engine

The second core mechanic: **hostages are your engine**. When you capture enemy units in combat, you can hold them. Each turn, you must return 1-3 hostages to earn your moves. Every aggressive action fuels your next turns, but simultaneously reinforces your opponent's army.

> "What if every aggressive move also strengthened your opponent?"

Capture aggressively and you fuel your next few turns, but those returned hostages land back on the board as enemy reinforcements. Starve your opponent of hostages and they cannot move, but neither can you, because you have nothing to return. The equilibrium shifts constantly.

It is simple enough to explain in a few sentences, but generates decisions that both chess and poker players would recognise as meaningful. This is the design philosophy of emergent complexity from minimal rules that we explored in [Conquering the Constraints of Chess](/news/conquering-the-constrains-of-chess/).

## The Catan conversion

To play Nukes with your Catan set:

1. Use 19 hex tiles as your board (any configuration)
2. Assign biomes to terrain: Fields, Forest, Mountains, Desert, Clay Pits
3. Use settlements or roads as tokens (one colour per player)
4. Download the rulebook from the [game page](/games/nukes/)

No new components. No expansions. No purchases. The game you already own now contains a second game inside it.

## How it compares

Where Nukes sits in the lineage of deterministic strategy:

- **[Checkers](https://rules.moddable.games/draughts/)** — 2 piece types, fixed board, more games than atoms in the universe
- **[Go](https://rules.moddable.games/go/)** — one piece type, one rule, more possible games than any other system
- **[Chess](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/)** — 6 unique movers on the same grid, lands somewhere between the two
- **Catan** — dice, 8 terrains, up to 6 players, billions of outcomes
- **Nukes** — 2 components, modular board, no theoretical ceiling
